## Phase 3.54 — Business-day durable timers: `ctx.timer_business_days` (issue #806)

**Implemented.** New author-facing primitive `WorkflowContext::timer_business_days(timer_id, n, calendar) -> HarvestResult<DateTime<Utc>>` arms a durable timer that resolves after `n` **business days** per a named calendar, stepping over weekends and the calendar's holidays — the one-call answer to "escalate this ticket after 2 business days", which `ctx.timer(id, 2 * 86_400)` gets wrong the moment the window straddles a weekend or a public holiday. A non-suspending sibling `business_days_from_now(id, n, calendar)` resolves and freezes the same deadline **without** arming a timer (for recording or returning a business-day date rather than waiting on it). **`autumn-harvest` core only** — no plugin, CLI, route, or contract-registry change.

**Invariants — no new event variant, no migration.** The resolved deadline is frozen into the **existing** `SideEffectRecorded { kind: Custom, name: "__harvest_business_day:{seq}:{id}" }` variant (issue #384) and the wait is carried by the **existing** `TimerStarted` event. The append-only invariant, the adjacently-tagged event JSON contract, and the shard model are all untouched.

**Three issue premises refuted and reconciled** (the #543/#593/#609 prior-art precedent — the ACs' *intent* is met, their literal mechanism could not be):

- **R-A — "persisted in the existing `TimerStarted.fires_at` field".** `WorkflowEvent::TimerStarted` has only `{ timer_id, duration_secs }`; there is **no** `fires_at` field on the event (the absolute instant lives solely on the `harvest_timers.fires_at` *column*). Reconciled by freezing the resolved deadline into the existing `SideEffectRecorded` variant and letting `TimerStarted.duration_secs` carry the resolved wait. The AC's actual bar — no new variant, no migration, frozen-on-replay — is fully met.
- **R-B — "skipping the calendar's weekend config".** No weekend column exists on `harvest_calendars`; the scheduler infers weekends from the `"weekends-off"` calendar *name* (`calendar_excludes_weekends`). Reconciled: for this primitive Sat/Sun are **always** non-business days whatever the calendar is named, and the named calendar contributes *holidays* only. Scoped strictly to this primitive — `apply_skip_policy` and the scheduler are untouched.
- **R-C — "time-of-day anchoring follows the calendar's default config".** No such config exists. Reconciled: business days are counted on **UTC dates** and the deadline preserves the anchor's UTC time-of-day (the same convention the `DATE`-typed exclusion rows and `rebase_to_date` already use). DST-free. **Known limitation:** a deployment whose local business day is offset from UTC is off by one business day near the UTC-midnight boundary, and for `n = 0` the roll-forward can be suppressed entirely (a local Saturday morning east of UTC is still a UTC Friday, so `n = 0` fires immediately rather than rolling to the local Monday) — both pinned by `utc_midnight_boundary_anchor_shifts_a_business_day`; a per-call timezone is a named follow-up.

**Determinism — the calendar is read once, ever.** The resolution runs on the **first live execution** from an anchor captured *inside* the freeze closure (never a fresh `Utc::now()` on replay, never its own `Now` side-effect event). Every later replay returns the recorded value verbatim and **never re-runs the resolution**, so an operator adding a holiday after a timer is armed can never move that timer's deadline (the snapshot lookup still runs on every call; its result is discarded on replay because `side_effect`'s matched arm never invokes the closure) — only timers armed afterwards see the edit. Proven by `replay_business_day_history_with_mutated_calendar_keeps_frozen_deadline` and the success-metric sweep `replay_business_day_1000_distinct_fixtures_all_succeed` (1000 **distinct** fixtures, each replayed under a *hostile* calendar that excludes that fixture's own frozen deadline date — 100% `ReplaySucceeded`).

**One decision cycle.** The freeze and the timer arm ride a **single** suspension batch (`[RecordSideEffect, StartTimer]`), which `extract_started_timer_for_suspension`'s bookkeeping allow-list already accepts. Pinned by the unit test `extract_started_timer_for_suspension_accepts_freeze_plus_timer` and, end to end, by the DB test asserting exactly one `SideEffectRecorded`, exactly one `TimerStarted`, and exactly one `harvest_timers` row. Deleting `RecordSideEffect` from that allow-list makes both DB tests fail (mutant-verified).

**Calendar registration — `ctx.state`, no new mechanism.** Holidays are resolved on the *worker* from a `BusinessCalendars` snapshot in builder shared state (`HarvestBuilder::state(BusinessCalendars::builtin())`) — no global static, no background refresher, no `WorkerConfig` knob, no new `WorkflowContext` field, no per-call DB read. New pure (non-`db`-gated) surface in `calendar.rs`, all re-exported from `lib.rs`: `is_business_day`, `add_business_days`, `add_business_days_bounded`, `next_business_day_at_or_after`, `BusinessDayResolution`, `BusinessDayRejection`, `MAX_BUSINESS_DAYS` (3650, ~14 calendar years), and `BusinessCalendars` (`new`/`builtin`/`with_calendar`/`with_calendar_covering`/`get`/`coverage_end`/`names`, plus `FromIterator`).

**Coverage horizon is declared, not inferred** — a design defect the RED phase exposed. An early cut inferred a calendar's horizon from its maximum exclusion date, which false-positived "coverage exhausted" for any resolution past the last holiday (caught by `timer_business_days_skips_a_holiday` failing). Split into `with_calendar` (**no** declared horizon — never rejected on coverage grounds) and `with_calendar_covering(name, holidays, coverage_end)` (declared horizon — a resolution needing a later date is **rejected**, never silently answered weekends-only, because a silently-wrong SLA date is worse than a loud failure). The shipped built-ins declare `BusinessCalendars::BUILTIN_COVERAGE_END` (2026-12-31).

**Error taxonomy — deployment errors are retryable, resolution outcomes are frozen.** **Prologue** errors record **zero** commands and are retryable after a redeploy: invalid `n` (over `MAX_BUSINESS_DAYS`) → `Config`; timer-id namespace collision with a live `ctx.start_timer` handle → `Config` (checked only on the timer-arming path — `business_days_from_now` arms no timer and records into a disjoint namespace, so rejecting it there would force a rename for no reason); and, **on a fresh dispatch**, no `BusinessCalendars` snapshot registered → `Config` **or** a calendar *name* absent from the registered snapshot → `NotFound` (never a panic, never a silent fire-now). Those last two are the same class of deployment misconfiguration — a typo'd name and a missing registration are both fixed by a redeploy — so they get the same retryable treatment; freezing the name error would durably poison every in-flight execution and force a per-execution workflow reset even after the operator corrected the registration (review finding). **Frozen** errors are recorded and replay identically forever (recover via workflow reset): coverage/scan exhaustion → `Config`, which genuinely depends on the captured anchor and which a catching workflow has already branched on. **`BusinessCalendars::builtin()` is dated**: its declared horizon (2026-12-31) means that as wall-clock time nears it, every `builtin()`-backed resolution freezes a coverage rejection fleet-wide, and shipping later arrays does not recover already-frozen executions — the rustdoc and the getting-started chapter now say so and point production at an operator-owned DB-loaded calendar. The prologue ordering is load-bearing and comment-marked **DO NOT REORDER**: every validation that is not a deterministic function of the frozen record must run *before* the `side_effect` call, or a rejected call would leave an orphaned `SideEffectRecorded` with no matching consumer on the next replay cycle.

**Canary-safety fix the money test caught.** The prologue's snapshot check initially ran on the **replay** path too, so a replay or deploy-replay-canary context — which legitimately holds empty shared state by design — failed with `no BusinessCalendars snapshot is registered`. Fixed with the new **non-consuming, cursor-safe** `HistoryMatcher::history_contains_custom_side_effect(name)` (modelled on the existing `history_contains_timer_started`), gating the check on `!already_frozen` so it only ever fires on a genuinely fresh dispatch. Mutant-verified: removing `!already_frozen &&` re-breaks exactly `replay_business_day_history_with_no_calendars_registered_succeeds`.

**`n = 0` is defined, not undefined**: it rolls forward — fires at the anchor when the anchor's UTC date is a business day, otherwise at the next business date at the same time-of-day. It never means "one business day later".

**Composition.** Sequential composition is supported (arm, await, arm the next — each with its own timer id; the second window anchors at the wall clock of the decision cycle that dispatches it, which is at or after the first deadline — so a continuation that crosses UTC midnight can land one business day later than a naive "anchored exactly at the first deadline" reading; the test harness's virtual clock does anchor exactly there). Racing a business-day timer against `receive_signal_timeout` in a **single** suspension is out of scope per the issue and rejected loudly by the engine's existing one-`StartTimer`-per-batch rule rather than silently mis-armed. Business-day timers are fire-once, like `ctx.timer`/`ctx.sleep_until`; there is no cancellable variant today.

**Test-harness seam.** `WorkflowTestEnv::with_business_calendars(BusinessCalendars)` (sugar over `with_state`) and `with_frozen_anchor(DateTime<Utc>)` fire business-day timers deterministically **without real sleeping** and make tests hermetic across all seven weekday anchors (rather than resolving differently depending on the day CI happens to run). The anchor reads the existing test-gated advancing virtual clock via a new private `business_day_anchor()`, mirroring `deadline_frontier_now_millis`'s precedent (#772) — so **no new `WorkflowContext` field** and none of the ~10 constructors were touched.

**Tests, TDD red→green→refactor.** 28 new pure unit tests in `calendar.rs` (bringing that module's non-`db` test count to 39 — weekend/holiday predicates, `n = 0` roll-forward, multi-week spans, the declared-vs-absent horizon split, overflow/scan bounds, and an agreement test pinning that the new `BTreeSet`-based `is_business_day` cannot drift from the pre-existing O(n) `is_excluded_impl`); 18 new unit tests in `context.rs` (prologue ordering, both error classes, frozen-record round-trips, the UTC-midnight boundary limitation); 6 replayer money tests in `tests/integration/business_day_replay_tests.rs` (calendar-withheld replay, mutated-calendar replay, the 1000-fixture determinism sweep, missing-freeze divergence, renamed-freeze `SideEffectMismatch`, frozen-rejection survival); 3 `WorkflowTestEnv` tests (incl. a hostile-calendar replay that is the harness-side freeze proof — `replay_check` forwards the env's shared state, so it is a round-trip check, not a freeze proof); 1 worker extractor pin; and 2 DB/worker integration tests in `tests/integration/business_day_timer_tests.rs` — the falsifiable correctness AC (holidays placed on the next two **weekdays** after the worker's real anchor so they genuinely enter the resolution window on every anchor weekday — a naive `today+1`/`+2` puts both on the weekend when CI runs on a Friday, making the falsifiability assertion unsatisfiable, asserted against an **independent** reference implementation written differently from the production arithmetic, plus an explicit falsifiability assertion that the holidays pushed the deadline later than a weekends-only resolution) and the DB-loaded-calendar path (`create_calendar` → `replace_calendar_exclusions` → `load_exclusions_for_calendar` → snapshot). Both DB tests are hermetic and re-runnable (per-run calendar names) and were **executed green against a real local Postgres 16**. Three mutants confirm the suite is load-bearing rather than vacuous: disabling holiday-skipping fails both DB tests; dropping `RecordSideEffect` from the timer-suspension allow-list fails both; moving the prologue collision check below the freeze fails the zero-command test.

**Docs and example.** `docs/getting-started/03-durable-timers.md` gains a "Business-day timers" section (registration, semantics, the determinism rule, the two-class error table, and a decision table relating the primitive to `ctx.timer`, `ctx.sleep_until` #749, cancellable timers #768, and calendar-aware *schedules* #337). Worked example `autumn-harvest/examples/business_day_escalation.rs` with 4 embedded tests, wired into CI.
