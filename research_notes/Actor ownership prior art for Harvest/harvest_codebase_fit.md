# Harvest codebase fit: owner-per-shard/key with colocated embedded storage

Scope: the Autumn Harvest repository at `/home/user/autumn-harvest` as of 2026-10-19 (branch `trunk-dev` lineage). Every source below is a repository file. Links are relative to this notes file. Line numbers were read in this session. The proposal under test has three stages: (1) shard ownership leases with a Postgres epoch, hashing `(workflow_name, workflow_id)` to a fixed number of logical shards, with all workflow decisions of a shard routed to its owner and activities kept on shared queues; (2) Postgres `harvest_events` stays the durable log, and the owner keeps a local SQLite cache rebuilt from a snapshot plus the log tail; (3) optional per-entity user SQLite, committed atomically with history.

Vocabulary warning for the report writer: in this codebase a **"shard" is a separate Postgres database**, not a logical partition with an owner ([docs/sharding.md:5](../../docs/sharding.md); [docs/architecture.md:345](../../docs/architecture.md)). The proposal's "shard" is a logical ownership unit. Inside one Harvest shard database, nobody owns an execution today; any eligible worker claims any task.

## Q1. How are workflow tasks claimed today, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway
Workflow and activity tasks share one Postgres table, `harvest_task_queue`, and one fused CTE claim with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. Workflow affinity is only a 5 s sticky lease plus an in-process LRU and "resident" parked futures. After issue #1971 the claim is flat with depth (about 4 to 9 ms). Throughput is bounded by claim count × claim latency, with 7 claims per 3-activity workflow. Four of those 7 claims are workflow-task claims, and an owner model would remove them. The two-claim-loop default (#2106) measurably adds extra cold decisions. Owner serialization would remove that effect.

### Cited Findings
- The queue module claims with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, "no two workers will ever claim the same task"; `TaskType` is `Workflow` or `Activity` in the same table — [autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs:1-36](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs)
- The claim is one SQL statement built from macros: leading CTEs (worker labels, paused queues, paused activities, concurrency keys), a bounded "seek window" candidate scan unioned with a legacy full-scan fallback, and `LIMIT 1 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` — [queue.rs:1778-1800](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs), [queue.rs:1284-1306](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs), [queue.rs:1328-1345](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs)
- Entry points: `claim_task` (2084), `claim_task_on_shard` (2124), `claim_task_of_kind_on_shard` (2158, one kind per issue #1787), `claim_task_with_fairness` (2225), by-id claim (2563) — [queue.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs)
- Sticky predicate inside the claim: `sticky_worker_id IS NULL OR sticky_worker_id = $1 OR sticky_until IS NULL OR sticky_until <= NOW()` — [queue.rs:1114-1121, 1160-1167](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs)
- Sticky routing is a "hard exclusion during the lease window, not a soft ordering hint". On by default with a 5 s window (#1798). A cache hit loads only the delta events since the last suspension. A hit removes the entry from the cache, and a gap in event ids drops it — [docs/sticky-routing.md:3, 13-19, 52](../../docs/sticky-routing.md)
- Resident workflows (#1798 step 2) keep the parked handler future and context. The next decision on the same worker sends the result to the future and does not replay. Only a narrow suspension set qualifies: one activity, timer or signal await, or a join of activities (#2008). No open race, park token, push signal handler, held mutex or cancel request. Anything else "declines" and replays cold — [docs/sticky-routing.md:21-39](../../docs/sticky-routing.md); [autumn-harvest/src/resident.rs:1-77](../../autumn-harvest/src/resident.rs)
- Resident state "lives only in worker memory. A restart, a failover, an LRU eviction or a decision that does not commit drops it." — [docs/sticky-routing.md:45](../../docs/sticky-routing.md)
- Failover: after a crash, each pinned execution waits up to one sticky window (5 s default). A graceful drain releases pins with one `UPDATE` per pool that "at worst scans all RUNNING rows of the task queue" — [docs/sticky-routing.md:83-90](../../docs/sticky-routing.md)
- Decision cost: cold replay 0.13 / 0.61 / 1.25 ms at 1k / 5k / 10k events. A resident decision costs 2.6 to 2.8 µs, flat — [docs/sticky-routing.md:113-117](../../docs/sticky-routing.md)
- Cache: a bounded LRU, 1,000 entries by default, bounded by count rather than bytes. A resident entry holds two copies of history (cache and context) — [docs/sticky-routing.md:121-129](../../docs/sticky-routing.md); [autumn-harvest/src/cache.rs:33-51](../../autumn-harvest/src/cache.rs)
- Claim concurrency (#2106): a leader poll loop plus up to `max_concurrent_claims - 1` followers (default 2). "No SQL change." Eager activity dispatch (B5), where a workflow task claims its own new activity rows, was deferred because "it changes claim ownership and needs its own fence review" — [DESIGN-claim-concurrency.md:10-11, 33, 36, 72-94](../../DESIGN-claim-concurrency.md)
- Assay #14: claim-loop occupancy was 0.91 to 0.99 in all 72 runs. "Throughput then follows `1 / (claims per workflow × claim latency)`, with 7 claims per workflow." The pool was not the bottleneck: 2 to 4 of 32 connections in use. Persist means stayed at 4 to 6 ms — [docs/assays/0014-harvest-vs-temporal-0.7.0-depth-sweep.md:199-216](../../docs/assays/0014-harvest-vs-temporal-0.7.0-depth-sweep.md)
- After #1971 and #2106 (one box, 3-activity workflow): 1 loop 32 to 35 workflows/s; 2 loops 41.8 to 44.5; 4 loops 48.2 to 51.2; Temporal 31.9 to 37.3. Mean claim time rises from about 4 ms (1 loop) to 6 ms (2) and 9 ms (4), and each loop holds about one more connection — [docs/performance.md:1690-1737](../../docs/performance.md)
- Seek-window claim (#1971): flat p50 of 6 to 12 ms from 1K to 1M pending rows at 8 claimers, 372 to 994 claims/s. The old full scan fell to 0.6 claims/s at 1M — [docs/performance.md:1421-1440](../../docs/performance.md); TL;DR at [docs/performance.md:44-52](../../docs/performance.md)
- The sticky-routing predicate itself costs +18.9% buffers at 1K rows, +32.9% at 10K and +36.2% at 100K. The cost is row width and MVCC, and "no query-shape fix" exists — [docs/performance-sticky-routing.md:1-30](../../docs/performance-sticky-routing.md)
- Resident hit rate (#2007, counter from #2114): e2e `throughput` 74.0% hits with 2 claim loops against 75.0% with 1 loop, and 43.4 against 30.8 workflows/s. The agent loop reached 89.6% to 96.2%. With two claim loops the bench made 64 extra decisions, all `cold`, and each one persisted. The suspected cause, untested: "a second workflow task for a run is claimed while the first decision holds the cache entry" — [docs/rnd/2026-10-11-resident-hit-rate.md:74-80, 104-117, 126-128, 137](../../docs/rnd/2026-10-11-resident-hit-rate.md)
- Parked workflow-task rows: a signal park is `RUNNING` with no worker. A timer park is `PENDING` with a future `scheduled_at`. `wake_workflow_task` resets a parked row to `PENDING` and refreshes `sticky_until`. A `wake_requested` flag catches a wake that races a park — [autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs:7130-7146, 7431-7446](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs); [docs/sharding.md:744-748](../../docs/sharding.md)
- The workflow-task claim is fenced on `(worker_id, attempt)` plus `crash_strikes` (`claim_still_held_for_update`, #1806). Deadlock and panic requeues of workflow tasks are fenced too (#1797, #1815) — [docs/architecture.md:186-221](../../docs/architecture.md)
- Redis dispatch (#1312) is a hint channel only. A worker still claims the named row in Postgres by id, at about 9.6 ms. After #1971 the default Postgres mode (21 to 22 workflows/s) beat the Redis mode (13.6 to 14.1) — [autumn-harvest-redis/src/lib.rs:61-77](../../autumn-harvest-redis/src/lib.rs); [assay 0014:189-193](../../docs/assays/0014-harvest-vs-temporal-0.7.0-depth-sweep.md)

### Inferences
- Stage 1 attacks the measured bottleneck directly. On the assay shape, 4 of 7 claims (start plus one per activity result) are workflow-task claims. If an owner drives decisions from an in-memory inbox, those claims leave the SKIP LOCKED path, and activity claims remain. The model `1/(claims × latency)` then predicts a large gain, but the persist (4 to 6 ms) and the activity claims remain. This is an extrapolation from the formula, not a measurement.
- The #2106 extra-cold-decision finding is the symptom a single owner per execution removes by construction: one decision per execution at a time on one process.
- Sticky routing is already a soft owner per execution with a 5 s TTL and no fence. A shard owner generalises it: one lease per logical shard, not one pin per task row. It also removes the +19% to +36% sticky predicate cost from the claim, if workflow tasks leave the shared claim.

### Gaps
- No measurement exists of workflow-task claims against activity-task claims in isolation, or of the decision-persist cost split (events against task rows against notify).
- The cause of the #2106 extra cold decisions is unconfirmed ([resident-hit-rate:126-128](../../docs/rnd/2026-10-11-resident-hit-rate.md)).
- No fleet hit-rate data exists. All rates come from a single worker with no eviction.

## Q2. Existing sharding, partitioning, ownership, leases, epochs and fencing

### Takeaway
Harvest has every building block except a per-logical-shard decision owner. It has: physical shards with a rendezvous hash over `(workflow_name, workflow_id)`; a per-database write-authority epoch with a `FOR SHARE` commit-order barrier (DR fence); a per-shard leader lease with an epoch that is deliberately not a fence (scanner leases); per-claim fencing tokens; mutex fencing sequences; and hard per-worker pins (sessions). No design document proposes decision ownership. The closest rejected idea is eager activity dispatch (B5).

### Cited Findings
- **Physical shards.** A shard is an independent Postgres database. One workflow's events, task rows, timers, signals and DLQ entries live on one shard, so "per-workflow ACID guarantees are preserved without cross-shard transactions" — [docs/sharding.md:5](../../docs/sharding.md)
- The shard id sits in the first two bytes of the `ExecutionId` (`ExecutionId::new_for_shard`). `UNENCODED = 0xFFFF` routes to the default shard — [docs/architecture.md:349](../../docs/architecture.md); [autumn-harvest/src/types.rs:98, 203, 221](../../autumn-harvest/src/types.rs)
- `ShardRouter::pick_for_new_workflow(workflow_name, workflow_id)` uses `seahash` rendezvous hashing over `readable_shards`, re-hashed onto `writable_shards`. Adding a shard moves about 1/N of keys — [autumn-harvest/src/shard.rs:235-242, 846, 1037](../../autumn-harvest/src/shard.rs); [docs/architecture.md:350](../../docs/architecture.md); [docs/sharding.md:105](../../docs/sharding.md)
- Residency keys map through an operator-declared map, never a hash, because a hash is not stable when the shard set widens — [docs/sharding.md:94-119](../../docs/sharding.md)
- Per-shard worker poll loops (`Worker::run_multi_shard`) rotate the start shard. `shard_assignments` empty means "every shard this process has a pool for" — [docs/architecture.md:366-368](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **Scanner leases (#1795).** One row per `(shard_id, scanner)` in `harvest_scanner_leases`, with `holder`, `lease_until` on the database clock and an `epoch` that "counts changes of holder". Acquire is an `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE ... WHERE l.holder = EXCLUDED.holder OR l.lease_until <= clock_timestamp() RETURNING l.epoch`. Default TTL is 10 s, the maximum 300 s, and a lease must cover 3 ticks. "The lease is a load control, not a fence", and the lease fails open — [autumn-harvest/src/scanner_lease.rs:1-35, 41-63, 440-475](../../autumn-harvest/src/scanner_lease.rs); [docs/architecture.md:1900](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **DR write-authority fence (#954, #1823).** `harvest_shard_generation` holds one monotonic epoch per shard database. A worker pins the epoch once at startup and "must never adopt a newer epoch". Claim gate: every `claim_task*` cross-joins the generation row, so a stale worker selects zero candidates. Persist assert: "every append takes the generation row `FOR SHARE` first"; the bump takes it exclusively, which makes "a commit-order barrier, not a racy read". Failure is `HarvestError::ShardFenced`, and a fenced attempt appends no event — [docs/cross-region-dr.md:294-320, 437-461](../../docs/cross-region-dr.md); [autumn-harvest/src/replication.rs:1-60](../../autumn-harvest/src/replication.rs); fence SQL in the claim at [queue.rs:2019](../../autumn-harvest/src/queue.rs); `assert_fence` before the execution row lock at [store.rs:1006-1009](../../autumn-harvest/src/store.rs); `assert_fence_group` at [replication.rs:2355](../../autumn-harvest/src/replication.rs)
- The DR doc rejected "worker leases with a TTL" for that problem because "the lease lives in the database that just failed over" — [docs/cross-region-dr.md:459](../../docs/cross-region-dr.md)
- **Request shard fence (#1803).** A task-local set of shards one management API request may reach, distinct from the write-authority fence — [autumn-harvest/src/shard_fence.rs:1-32](../../autumn-harvest/src/shard_fence.rs)
- **Activity claim epoch (#1789).** `(worker_id, attempt)` is a fencing token. `claim_held` (`state = 'RUNNING' AND worker_id = $w AND attempt = $a`) guards every owner write. TLA+ model `formal/tla/ActivityClaim.tla` and a deterministic simulation (`autumn_harvest::dst`) check it — [docs/architecture.md:186-235](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **Durable mutex (#691).** Shard-local lock rows with a lease TTL (60 s default) and a monotonic `lock_seq` fencing token. "What the engine cannot fence is the author's external side effects." — [autumn-harvest/src/mutex.rs:1-39](../../autumn-harvest/src/mutex.rs); lease reclaim cost at [docs/performance-mutex-lease-reclaim.md:1-25](../../docs/performance-mutex-lease-reclaim.md)
- **Worker sessions (#606).** `harvest_sessions.host_worker_id` hard-pins member activities to one worker. Unlike sticky routing, the pin "never fails over" — [docs/architecture.md:1894](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **Worker liveness.** `harvest_workers` registration and heartbeat. Poison-pill reclaim requeues orphaned `RUNNING` rows of dead workers and counts `crash_strikes`. A second pass catches a stuck `RUNNING` workflow task on a live worker (#1459) — [autumn-harvest/src/poison_pill.rs:1-30](../../autumn-harvest/src/poison_pill.rs); [workers.rs:299, 546](../../autumn-harvest/src/workers.rs)
- **Tenant cells (#1837).** A cell is one shard plus one worker pool, built from residency keys, `with_reserved_shards` and `with_shard_assignments` — [docs/sharding.md:418-475](../../docs/sharding.md); [docs/adr/0004-tenant-isolation-cells.md](../../docs/adr/0004-tenant-isolation-cells.md)
- **Shard rebalancing (#964).** It moves quiescent executions only: "copy ──▶ replay-verify ──▶ ONE atomic cutover commit ──▶ sealed source". The `ExecutionId` never changes. New states are `MIGRATING` (target) and `MIGRATED` (source, with a forwarding pointer). It needs no catch-up phase, dual write or 2PC — [docs/sharding.md:638-720](../../docs/sharding.md)
- **Redis.** One `RedisDispatch` channel per shard database. A stream entry is a task reference, Postgres stays the source of truth, and "sticky affinity is best effort" — [autumn-harvest-redis/src/lib.rs:61-130](../../autumn-harvest-redis/src/lib.rs)
- **Partitioning of `harvest_events` (#958)** is a physical layout keyed on an append-instant `cohort`. It is for drop-based retention and has no relation to ownership — [docs/partitioned-events.md:1-10, 86-143](../../docs/partitioned-events.md)

### Inferences
- A shard-ownership lease can copy `scanner_lease.rs`'s acquire SQL (holder, epoch, `clock_timestamp()` TTL) and add the DR fence's barrier: each decision persist takes the owner-lease row `FOR SHARE` and checks the pinned epoch, and a takeover bumps the epoch under an exclusive lock. Both halves exist; only their combination is new.
- The DR fence's rule "never adopt a newer epoch; restart" is too strict for routine lease churn. An owner must be able to drop a shard and re-acquire it at a new epoch without a process restart. The fence must therefore be per logical shard and per acquisition, not per process.
- The proposal's hash `(workflow_name, workflow_id)` to a fixed shard count matches the existing rendezvous input. A fixed logical count also avoids the 1/N remap that rendezvous causes on a physical shard add. Logical shards would then nest inside physical shards: logical shard → physical database, via a map.

### Gaps
- No repository document proposes or rejects a decision-owner model. The only adjacent decision is B5 (eager activity dispatch), deferred for a fence review.

## Q3. Transactional invariants that ownership would change

### Takeaway
Today correctness rests on Postgres row locks and unique constraints, not on who runs the decision. Any process may append to any history. The appenders serialize on the execution row (`FOR UPDATE` plus `MAX(event_id)`), and `UNIQUE(workflow_exec_id, event_id)` is "the engine's optimistic-concurrency detector". Many writers other than the decision path append events: activity workers, timers, signals, child completions, scanners, operator APIs and outboxes. Many cross-run features need the decision transaction and other runs' rows in one shard database. An owner per logical shard must keep all of these in one Postgres transaction. So stage 1 changes who decides, and must not change where commits happen.

### Cited Findings
- **One decision commits in one transaction.** `persist_workflow_outcome` dispatches to leaf persists that take the caller's connection inside a transaction ("the caller is running inside a transaction") — [autumn-harvest/src/worker.rs:24199-24250](../../autumn-harvest/src/worker.rs). Leaf persists cover activity scheduling, timers, child starts, signal and mutex parks, mixed suspension batches, search attributes, current details, logs, durable streams and continue-as-new — [worker.rs:9550-24170](../../autumn-harvest/src/worker.rs) (function list: `persist_scheduled_activities` 11764, `persist_started_timer` 11922, `persist_all_started_child_workflows` 12193, `persist_mixed_suspension_batch` 13975, `persist_search_attrs_from_commands` 10459, `persist_signal_wait_park` 10770, `persist_mutex_acquire_park` 10998, `persist_workflow_continue_as_new` 24170)
- A decision ends with a `DecisionCommitted` event in the same transaction. "Other writers also add events between two boundaries: activity workers, timers and signals." — [docs/decision-boundaries.md:8-25](../../docs/decision-boundaries.md)
- **Per-execution append serialization.** `append_single_event_with_codecs` runs `assert_fence`, then `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on `harvest_workflow_executions`, then `MAX(event_id)+1`. Its stated purpose: serialize "concurrent appenders (management API paths, timeout enforcement)". `next_event_id_for` takes the same lock for each decision boundary — [autumn-harvest/src/store.rs:976-1090](../../autumn-harvest/src/store.rs)
- `append_events` fails with a unique violation when "two workers tried to advance the same workflow simultaneously" — [store.rs:156-172](../../autumn-harvest/src/store.rs). The partition design calls `UNIQUE (workflow_exec_id, event_id)` "the engine's optimistic-concurrency detector" — [docs/partitioned-events.md:100-104, 112-118](../../docs/partitioned-events.md)
- **Child completion writes the parent's history** in the child's transaction: it appends `ChildWorkflowCompleted/Failed` to the parent and wakes the parent's task, unless the parent is on another shard — [worker.rs:15560-15580, 15584](../../autumn-harvest/src/worker.rs)
- **Cross-shard children** use a lifecycle row in `harvest_cross_shard_children`, written in the parent's decision transaction. A scanner relays it at least once with dedupe, and the terminal notify is a pull. "The parent's decision transaction stays shard-local. Always." — [docs/sharding.md:576-597](../../docs/sharding.md)
- **Signal-with-start** collapses fetch, start and signal "into one shard-local transaction" — [docs/architecture.md:527-533](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **Completion triggers**: a same-shard target start is inserted "within the same database transaction that persists the source"; a cross-shard target uses an outbox with dedupe — [docs/completion-triggers.md:10-35](../../docs/completion-triggers.md)
- **Completion callbacks** enqueue "inside the same terminal transaction as every other" terminal write — [docs/completion-callbacks.md:140](../../docs/completion-callbacks.md)
- **Shard-local features** that rely on one database: per-key concurrency limits (claim counts `RUNNING` rows), latest-wins supersede (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext(K))` plus a scan of the shard's executions), debounce, per-key activity rate limits, durable mutex, per-tenant quotas, LLM budgets — [docs/sharding.md:18-70, 1116-1160](../../docs/sharding.md); [docs/architecture.md:390](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **Lock-order hazards** already exist between the latest-wins advisory lock and mutex locks. Postgres resolves them with `40P01`, and the workflow task is re-pended (#1822) — [docs/sharding.md:70-76](../../docs/sharding.md)
- **Transactional activities and starts** need user tables in the same Postgres database: `ctx.run_transactional` commits the domain write and `ActivityCompleted` in one transaction; `start_workflow_transactional` stages the start on the caller's connection — [docs/transactional-activities.md:7-50](../../docs/transactional-activities.md); [docs/transactional-start.md:1-25](../../docs/transactional-start.md)
- **Shard-local ties of one execution.** The rebalancing quiescence predicate lists them: a claimed or dispatchable workflow task, live activities, unconsumed signals, `INFLIGHT` completion deliveries, `ACTIVE` sessions, live children, a held or awaited mutex, DLQ rows, a schedule attribution, and fork lineage — [docs/sharding.md:714-742](../../docs/sharding.md)
- **Post-commit notify.** Every event append stages `notify_workflow_events_appended`, sent only after the write commits (#1796, #2125) — [autumn-harvest/src/notify.rs:1-30, 311-330](../../autumn-harvest/src/notify.rs); call sites [store.rs:203, 443, 514, 773](../../autumn-harvest/src/store.rs)

### Inferences
- Stage 1 is compatible with every invariant above if the owner still commits each decision as one Postgres transaction on the execution's physical shard, and the per-execution row lock and unique `event_id` stay as a backstop. Ownership then removes contention (two deciders) rather than adding safety. The epoch check at persist adds the safety for a stale owner.
- Activity completions, timer fires, signal sends, child-terminal appends, cancels and admin writes stay foreign appenders. The owner must learn about them through the log tail, for example through `notify_workflow_events_appended`, or through a wake routed to the owner. The resident delta check already rejects any delta it did not expect, and runs a cold replay instead ([resident.rs:28-40](../../autumn-harvest/src/resident.rs)).
- Cross-run features that read or lock other runs' rows, such as latest-wins, concurrency counts, mutex and quotas, keep working because they live in SQL on the physical shard. They do not care which process issued the transaction. A logical shard that maps to one physical shard keeps every same-shard path. Two logical shards on one physical shard can have different owners, so a decision may still write another owner's execution (a child terminal into its parent, a latest-wins cancel). That is safe under the row lock, but it invalidates the other owner's cache.

### Gaps
- No document enumerates the full transaction boundary of a decision in one place. The persist function list above is reconstructed from function names and doc comments, not from a traced transaction.

## Q4. The keyed entity, and what a per-key owner would improve

### Takeaway
The keyed entity (#1975) is sugar over a workflow: key = `workflow_id`, op = a `harvest.entity.op` signal through signal-with-start, state = workflow input rebuilt by replay, durability = periodic continue-as-new checkpoints. It adds no event variant, no migration and no worker path. A native primitive is ranked 13, "behind customer pull". A per-key owner would make the resident path the normal case for entities. It would not remove the continue-as-new checkpoint cost, because Postgres history stays the source of truth.

### Cited Findings
- Mapping: entity type = `#[workflow]`, key = `workflow_id`, operation = signal `harvest.entity.op`, send = `signal_with_start`, read = queries `harvest.entity.state` and `harvest.entity.stats`, durable state = "the workflow input, rebuilt by replay" — [docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md:29-41](../../docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md); `ENTITY_OP_SIGNAL` at [autumn-harvest/src/entity.rs:90](../../autumn-harvest/src/entity.rs), `Entity::run` at [entity.rs:355-402](../../autumn-harvest/src/entity.rs)
- Serialization per key: one active run per `(workflow_name, workflow_id)`. The loop takes one op, awaits its handler to the end, then takes the next. Ops run in `SignalReceived` order — [ADR 0006:55-62](../../docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md)
- Checkpointing: after each op the loop records `should_continue_as_new()`, its own op count or a byte estimate with `ctx.side_effect`, which costs one `SideEffectRecorded` event per op. At a checkpoint it carries waiting op signals in the continue-as-new input. The state must fit the workflow input cap — [ADR 0006:64-87, 109-112](../../docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md)
- Hazards that motivated the sugar: continue-as-new moves only un-ingested signals, and `should_continue_as_new()` read the growing loaded history (fixed in #2080) — [ADR 0006:16-23](../../docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md)
- Checkpoint hardening (#2049): the budget is recorded per attempt, and `last_error` is cut to 1,024 bytes — [docs/plans/2026-10-15-entity-checkpoint-hardening.md:1-60](../../docs/plans/2026-10-15-entity-checkpoint-hardening.md); [ADR 0006:120-126](../../docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md)
- Rejected alternatives: "New event variants and an entity table" (migration, trigger change, SQLite work); "state outside history behind `ctx.mutex(key)`" because "replay cannot rebuild the state" — [ADR 0006:155-164](../../docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md)
- Limits: no reply to an op (a caller reads state with a query); no entity as a child workflow; SQLite cannot run an entity because it has no queries — [ADR 0006:100-101, 113-114, 152-153](../../docs/adr/0006-keyed-entity.md)
- Queries are served by replay (`drive_query_replay`, `drive_query_replay_async`) — [autumn-harvest/src/executor.rs:743, 825](../../autumn-harvest/src/executor.rs)
- The typed-checkpoint verdict lists "a versioned state, an upgrade step and a cover rule for the keyed entity checkpoint" as follow-up 3 — [docs/rnd/typed-state-snapshots.md:273-281](../../docs/rnd/typed-state-snapshots.md)

### Inferences
- An entity loop is a single signal wait per op, which is the resident path's best case (agent loop hit rate 89.6% to 96.2%). On one warm worker an entity already avoids replay. An owner adds two things: the hit rate survives the 5 s sticky expiry for idle keys, and two workflow tasks for one key never race (the #2106 effect).
- An owner holding live state could answer `harvest.entity.state` queries from memory, not by replay, and could return an op reply. Both need a request path to the owner, which Harvest does not have (all routing is through Postgres rows).
- Stage 3 (per-entity SQLite) collides with the ADR's rejection of "state outside history". That rejection is unanswered unless the SQLite state is either derived deterministically from history or its writes are journalled into the Postgres history transaction.

### Gaps
- No measurement exists of entity throughput, per-op latency or checkpoint cost.

## Q5. The SQLite backend as an owner-local store, and the StorageBackend seam verdict

### Takeaway
`autumn-harvest-sqlite` is a separate single-writer runtime, not a cache. It reuses the determinism core "wholesale" and reimplements persistence. Its schema, append-only guard, single-writer OS lock and transactional finalize helpers are reusable pieces. Its drive model, feature subset and lack of a warm or delta path are not. The feasibility report (#966) concluded: no `StorageBackend` trait in core; a separate crate instead; and the single-writer constraint "must be re-opened, not extended" if multi-writer is ever needed.

### Cited Findings
- Decision: "Go — as a separate companion crate, not as a `StorageBackend` trait in core." The determinism core is already portable (plain `ExecutionId`, `Vec<WorkflowEvent>`, handler `fn`, JSON). The coordination layer is not: "Multi-worker claim, push notification, and cross-connection locking are the three load-bearing Postgres features" — [docs/rnd/sqlite-feasibility.md:39-55](../../docs/rnd/sqlite-feasibility.md)
- Seam sizing: an event store (about 6 methods, "trait-able cleanly"), a task queue (the hard part: exclusive and non-blocking claim), seven scanner loops on SKIP LOCKED, coordination primitives with defined lock ordering, about 2,500 lines of Diesel types, and 83 migrations. SQLite violates the non-blocking claim clause. "So the trait is buildable", but it costs too much — [sqlite-feasibility.md:268-342](../../docs/rnd/sqlite-feasibility.md)
- "Load-bearing constraint ... single writer, single process, one SQLite file ... If multi-writer edge deployment ever becomes a requirement, this decision must be re-opened, not extended." — [sqlite-feasibility.md:500-506](../../docs/rnd/sqlite-feasibility.md)
- Hub handoff (edge to Postgres) is "Not built. Not designed. Scoped only". Its blockers: `ExecutionId` shard bytes, conflicting `(workflow_name, workflow_id)` across nodes ("append-only histories do not merge"), and replay fidelity on ingest. libSQL/Turso is named as the first option if multi-writer is needed — [sqlite-feasibility.md:524-570](../../docs/rnd/sqlite-feasibility.md)
- A history written by SQLite is "byte-identical, per event" to Postgres and replays on `WorkflowReplayer` — [docs/sqlite-backend.md:38-47](../../docs/sqlite-backend.md)
- Schema: `harvest_events (exec_id, seq, event_json, PRIMARY KEY (exec_id, seq))`, plus `harvest_executions`, `harvest_tasks`, `harvest_timers`, `harvest_signals`, mutex tables, `harvest_schedules`, `harvest_activity_attempts` — [autumn-harvest-sqlite/src/schema.rs:58-329](../../autumn-harvest-sqlite/src/schema.rs)
- The SQLite append-only guard refuses every `UPDATE` and an insert over a stored key, and leaves `DELETE` open for retention. "It has no sanctioned writer" — [schema.rs:350-370](../../autumn-harvest-sqlite/src/schema.rs); [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) (SQLite backend paragraph)
- Single-writer lock (#1834): an exclusive OS `flock` on `<database>.lock`. The kernel releases it on crash. An in-memory database uses an owner row instead — [autumn-harvest-sqlite/src/lock.rs:1-30](../../autumn-harvest-sqlite/src/lock.rs); [docs/sqlite-backend.md:1128-1160](../../docs/sqlite-backend.md)
- `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` replaces SKIP LOCKED. WAL mode with `synchronous = FULL`. Open reclaims orphaned `RUNNING` tasks — [docs/sqlite-backend.md:1085-1125](../../docs/sqlite-backend.md)
- The claim and freeze share one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`. Bodies run outside any transaction (at least once). Finalize, timeout and timer fire are each one transaction — [autumn-harvest-sqlite/src/worker.rs:11-21, 99-160, 929-956, 1512-1530](../../autumn-harvest-sqlite/src/worker.rs)
- Drive model: polling (`poll_once`, `run_until_blocked`, `run_until_idle`), no push — [docs/sqlite-backend.md:276-345](../../docs/sqlite-backend.md)
- Capability losses, each rejected by name: multi-worker, push wakeups, per-key fleet concurrency, sticky routing, poison-pill peer reclaim, sharding, management API, external activities, worker sessions, cross-type continue-as-new; also `quota` declarations — [sqlite-feasibility.md:508-522](../../docs/rnd/sqlite-feasibility.md); [docs/architecture.md:395](../../docs/architecture.md)
- The SQLite decision cycle reloads and reparses the full history each cycle. That accounts for 70.25% of allocation bytes, and no warm or delta path exists — [docs/performance-sqlite-runtime-drive.md:1-25](../../docs/performance-sqlite-runtime-drive.md)

### Inferences
- Reusable for an owner-local cache: the SQLite event table shape and the JSON event encoding (byte-identical), `store.rs` append and load, the `lock.rs` single-writer sidecar (one owner process per local file), and the guard. A cache must be able to drop and rebuild rows, which `DELETE` permits.
- Not reusable: `SqliteRuntime` as the decision engine. It would fork the feature set and lacks the Postgres-only features above. The owner should run the Postgres worker's decision path and use SQLite only as a materialized view.
- Stage 2 does not need the rejected `StorageBackend` trait. The cache sits beside the Postgres path, like the existing `WorkflowCache` (an in-memory LRU), and is not a second source of truth. That matches the report's own recommendation pattern: reuse the core, do not abstract the coupled half.
- A local SQLite cache buys little over the in-memory `WorkflowCache` plus resident futures unless it must survive a process restart, or hold more histories than RAM allows. A cold replay costs about 1.25 ms at 10k events and about 6 ms at the 50k cap ([typed-state-snapshots.md:91-95](../../docs/rnd/typed-state-snapshots.md)). Disk persistence saves the Postgres history read on owner restart, not the replay CPU.

### Gaps
- No measurement exists of history load time from Postgres against local SQLite for long histories.

## Q6. What would invalidate an owner's local cache: every non-decision writer

### Takeaway
At least 13 modules append to `harvest_events` outside the owning decision, and two sanctioned writers rewrite stored rows in place: erasure (terminal only) and codec rotation (live runs too, ciphertext only). Many more write `harvest_workflow_executions`. The existing cache detects only appends, through a contiguous `event_id >= next_event_id` delta. A local cache that keeps decoded plaintext survives codec rotation. One that keeps ciphertext does not. Reset, terminate, cancel, pause, rebalance, partition layout changes and retention each need an explicit eviction or fence rule.

### Cited Findings
- Append call sites by file (workspace grep, tests excluded): `worker.rs` 53, `execution.rs` 13, `timeout.rs` 10, `cross_shard_child.rs` 4, `external_task.rs` 3, `reset.rs` 2, `poison_pill.rs` 2, `fork.rs` 2, `codec_rotation.rs` 2, `sessions.rs` 1, `context.rs` 1, `autumn-harvest-plugin/src/api.rs` 1. Append functions are in [store.rs:167-997](../../autumn-harvest/src/store.rs)
- `execution.rs` appenders (operator, API and cross-run): start-or-load (1354), `replace_execution` (3693), `inline_cancel` (3843), `notify_awaited_parent_of_child_terminal` (3957), `cancel_workflow_execution_collect_mode` (4138), `pause_workflow_execution` (5349), `resume_workflow_execution` (5788), `reactivate_failed_execution_at` (6171), `apply_parent_close_cascade` (6353), cascade cancel and terminate of detached children (6441, 6525), `terminate_workflow_execution_collect` (6612) — [autumn-harvest/src/execution.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/execution.rs)
- `timeout.rs` appenders (scanners): child-timeout parent wake (1907), workflow execution timeout (1942, 2966), activity timeout (2057), `force_fail_activity` (2778), external task timeouts (3215), external signals, cancels and awaits outboxes (4372, 4878, 5567), history ceiling enforcement (6865) — [autumn-harvest/src/timeout.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/timeout.rs)
- Other appenders: cross-shard child relay (start 1365, terminal 1865, cascade 1232) [cross_shard_child.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/cross_shard_child.rs); external task complete, fail and extend (334, 388, 442) [external_task.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/external_task.rs); session break failing members (770) [sessions.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/sessions.rs); reset source terminate and fork marker (1436, 1778) [reset.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/reset.rs); fork (306, 2196) [fork.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/fork.rs); poison-pill quarantine [poison_pill.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/poison_pill.rs); update admission `UpdateAdmitted` under the row lock [store.rs:1153-1165](../../autumn-harvest/src/store.rs)
- Activity workers append `ActivityStarted` and `ActivityCompleted` between decision boundaries — [docs/decision-boundaries.md:24-37](../../docs/decision-boundaries.md)
- Signals are staged in `harvest_signals` and a parked task is woken. Continue-as-new moves unconsumed signals to the successor — [autumn-harvest/src/signal.rs:17-60](../../autumn-harvest/src/signal.rs); [worker.rs:24140-24144](../../autumn-harvest/src/worker.rs)
- **In-place rewriter 1, PII erasure** (`erase.rs`, #495): terminal executions only (`TERMINAL_STATES`, [erase.rs:21, 149-177](../../autumn-harvest/src/erase.rs)). It replaces payload fields with `_harvest_erased` — [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md); [docs/architecture.md:148-152](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **In-place rewriter 2, codec rotation** (`codec_rotation.rs`, #948): re-encrypts ciphertext under the active key with a CAS `UPDATE harvest_events SET event_data = $1 WHERE id = $2 AND event_data = $3`. The decoded plaintext is byte-identical. A per-shard cursor sweeps by `last_event_id` — [codec_rotation.rs:49, 1179-1180, 1839](../../autumn-harvest/src/codec_rotation.rs); [docs/architecture.md:1896](../../docs/architecture.md). Rotation also rewrites `CODEC_COLUMNS` on non-event tables (#1979) — [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)
- **Append-only trigger** (#1817): `harvest_events_append_only_trg`, `BEFORE UPDATE`. `event_data` changes only under the `harvest.sanctioned_event_rewrite` GUC (`erase` or `codec_rotation`). The `type` key and all other columns, `cohort` included, never change. DELETE and TRUNCATE are not guarded — [docs/architecture.md:154-166](../../docs/architecture.md); [autumn-harvest/src/append_only.rs](../../autumn-harvest/src/append_only.rs)
- **Reset** terminates the source (`TERMINATED`, released from uniqueness) and forks a successor with a new `ExecutionId` on the same shard. Undelivered signals are dropped or buffered onto the fork — [reset.rs:1-45, 1436](../../autumn-harvest/src/reset.rs); [docs/architecture.md:549-553](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **Shard rebalance** copies the history to another database, verifies it by replay, then seals the source `MIGRATED` in one cutover commit. A wake during the copy aborts the cutover — [docs/sharding.md:638-760](../../docs/sharding.md); `shard_rebalance.rs` and `partition.rs` insert into `harvest_events` directly (grep for `INSERT INTO harvest_events`)
- **Partition layout changes** rebuild `harvest_events` with `LIKE`, and must reinstall the guard. `disable_partitioning` resets `cohort` to `-infinity`. Partition drops reclaim closed cohorts with no live owner — [docs/partitioned-events.md:145-175](../../docs/partitioned-events.md); [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)
- **Retention** deletes expired terminal executions (event rows go by `ON DELETE CASCADE`, or by partition drop). The opt-in `straggler_grace_secs` deletes orphan rows — [docs/partitioned-events.md:12-28, 289-305](../../docs/partitioned-events.md); [autumn-harvest/src/retention.rs:1](../../autumn-harvest/src/retention.rs)
- **Continue-as-new** patches `last_completion_result` into the successor's first event before its insert, so it is not an in-place rewrite — [docs/architecture.md:166](../../docs/architecture.md)
- **Existing cache detection**: a delta loads `event_id >= next_event_id`. The delta must "run on from the cached `next_event_id` with no gap", and a resident resume declines on `KeyChanged` (deadline, shard, build changed) and on `OwnEventsMismatch` — [cache.rs:38-51](../../autumn-harvest/src/cache.rs); [docs/sticky-routing.md:19](../../docs/sticky-routing.md); [resident.rs:86-110](../../autumn-harvest/src/resident.rs)
- Payload offload: the cache holds decoded payloads "with offloaded payloads loaded back in" — [docs/sticky-routing.md:127](../../docs/sticky-routing.md)

### Inferences
- Appends by foreign writers are the normal case, not an invalidation. A log-tail cache (stage 2) handles them the way `WorkflowCache` does today: load the delta, and replay cold on any shape it does not expect.
- Rewrites in place: erasure acts only on terminal runs, so the rule "evict on terminal" (the cache already does this for continue-as-new, [docs/sticky-routing.md:137](../../docs/sticky-routing.md)) covers it. A persisted local SQLite copy of a terminal history would retain erased PII unless the erase path also purges owner caches. That is a compliance hazard the in-memory LRU does not have.
- Codec rotation rewrites live runs. A cache of decoded plaintext stays correct by the rotation's own guarantee. A cache of ciphertext (an encrypted-at-rest SQLite copy) needs its own re-encryption, or the retired key cannot be destroyed while the copy exists.
- Terminal and identity changes (reset, terminate, cancel, rebalance cutover, retention, partition rebuild) change execution state, not only history. The owner must treat an execution-row state change as an eviction signal. Today `KeyChanged` and the cold-replay fallback cover in-memory state, and stage 2 needs the same rule for the disk copy.
- A new writer to `harvest_events` from the owner path is not needed. An owner that only appends through the existing `store::append_*` functions creates no new exception to the append-only invariant, so CLAUDE.md's exception list stays at two.

### Gaps
- `poison_pill.rs` and `context.rs` append sites were counted by grep, but their enclosing functions were not traced.
- No test or doc states how `WorkflowCache` reacts to an execution-row state change made by another process (for example a pause) beyond the delta and `KeyChanged` checks.

## Q7. Relevant prior R&D

### Takeaway
Four measured spikes bound the proposal. Typed state snapshots: no-go in place, go over continue-as-new. Resident hit rate: high on one worker; two claim loops add cold decisions. Contention-adaptive atomicity: one-transaction backout wins only for cold keys. Partitioned events: cohort, not ownership. The DR fence is the template for an epoch fence. Redis dispatch did not beat the Postgres claim once the claim was fixed.

### Cited Findings
- **Typed state snapshots (#2013)**: "no-go on an in-place snapshot now; go on typed checkpoints over continue-as-new". The resident path already removes replay from warm sequential decisions. A cold replay costs about 6 ms at the default 50,000-event cap, so "snapshots would buy little speed". An in-place snapshot needs a new event variant, a new resume path, saved context counters, a snapshot mode in each replay tool, and erasure and rotation coverage of the new state field. Verdict changers: `outcome="cold"` above 20% for a week, cold p99 above 50 ms, or child checkpoints and stable ids required — [docs/rnd/typed-state-snapshots.md:88-101, 109-137, 245-296](../../docs/rnd/typed-state-snapshots.md)
- **Resident hit rate**: see Q1. The next step is to find the cause of the extra cold decisions under two claim loops — [docs/rnd/2026-10-11-resident-hit-rate.md:135-138](../../docs/rnd/2026-10-11-resident-hit-rate.md)
- **Contention-adaptive atomicity (#2012)**: "Go with changes." Physical backout (one transaction, a savepoint per step) gives 1.55x the goodput of a saga for cold, short, same-database workflows (3,004 against 1,944 runs/s). Under a hot key, a saga or a hybrid wins by up to 3x (hot, 20 ms: backout 13.3 against hybrid 39.9 runs/s) — [docs/rnd/contention-adaptive-atomicity.md:17-36, 99-112, 130-140](../../docs/rnd/contention-adaptive-atomicity.md)
- **Partitioned events (#958)**: cohort = append instant floored to a day. Drop gate. Known limit: concurrent duplicate appends across a cohort boundary are detected by `harvest backup verify`, not prevented (ADR 0004 partitioned duplicate append detection) — [docs/partitioned-events.md:86-175](../../docs/partitioned-events.md)
- **Cross-region DR**: fence (Q2). Failover is manual: "isolating the old primary's database is a mandatory operator step". A bump on a healthy shard is "a fleet-wide outage recovered by restarting the fleet" — [docs/cross-region-dr.md:463-503](../../docs/cross-region-dr.md)
- **Redis**: dispatch channel per shard, reference only. After #1971 it lost to the Postgres claim (13.6 to 14.1 against 21 to 22 workflows/s) — [assay 0014:189-193](../../docs/assays/0014-harvest-vs-temporal-0.7.0-depth-sweep.md); deferral record at [docs/rnd/2026-09-03-redis-queue-worker-integration-deferral.md](../../docs/rnd/2026-09-03-redis-queue-worker-integration-deferral.md) (not read in full)
- **Performance agent rule**: do not profile the strict replay path again. The non-strict `match_activity` path is unmeasured — [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) "Performance Agents"

### Inferences
- The typed-snapshot verdict caps what stage 2's "snapshot plus log tail" can claim. Its value is restart and failover warmth, and cold-hit thresholds (20% cold, 50 ms p99) already exist as the decision gate. Stage 2 should be gated on the same fleet counter, `harvest.workflow.resident{outcome="cold"}`.
- Stage 3 (per-entity SQL committed with history) is the atomicity spike's "backout" arm moved off Postgres. The spike shows one-transaction atomicity pays only for cold keys. A per-entity owner makes every key single-writer, so hot-key lock waits disappear within one entity, but a cross-entity effect still needs a saga or an outbox.

### Gaps
- `docs/rnd/2026-09-03-redis-queue-worker-integration-deferral.md` and `docs/plans/2026-09-02-shard-rebalancing.md` were only searched, not read in full.

## Q8. Existing research notes on Restate, Golem, Durable Objects, actors and entities

### Takeaway
Earlier notes in this repo already describe the target architecture's prior art: Temporal history shards with one owner each, Restate partition processors with a materialized RocksDB cache, S3 snapshots and log trim, epochs that reject superseded attempts, and Golem per-agent SQLite. They classify Harvest as the "thin library over Postgres" family, with no keyed actors, no snapshots and no embedded KV store.

### Cited Findings
- Two families: "an engine owns a log or shard and keeps hot state close to compute" (Temporal history shards, Restate Bifrost and RocksDB partition processors) against "a thin library over Postgres" (DBOS and others) — [Durable workflow engine R&D opportunities/storage_scale_performance.md:8](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/storage_scale_performance.md)
- Temporal: the shard count is fixed at cluster creation; "a shard allows only one concurrent operation inside its partition at a time"; owning a shard means handling every execution's requests and background tasks — [storage_scale_performance.md:12-13](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/storage_scale_performance.md)
- Restate: partition processors tail the log and keep a materialized cache in embedded RocksDB (journals, idempotency metadata, virtual-object state, timer indices). They snapshot to S3 "a few times per hour", trim the log, and on takeover replay only the suffix. Vendor benchmark: 8,571 workflows/s on a 3-node cluster — [storage_scale_performance.md:19-24, 116-126](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/storage_scale_performance.md)
- Restate: "Attempts carry monotonically increasing epochs; the processor rejects any events from superseded epochs." Keys hash to one partition, so "no cross-partition coordination is needed" — [Autumn Harvest resilience gap analysis/execution_model.md:59](../Autumn%20Harvest%20resilience%20gap%20analysis/execution_model.md)
- Restate virtual objects: durable handlers plus isolated K/V state, single writer per key (Rust SDK) — [Durable workflow engine feature gaps/market_trends_requirements.md:141](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20feature%20gaps/market_trends_requirements.md); [code_first_engines.md:19-25](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20feature%20gaps/code_first_engines.md)
- Golem: per-agent oplog, suspend-to-zero, "a stateful sandbox per identity", per-agent filesystem and SQL ("available today to TypeScript agents"). An open issue tracks exactly-once for `golem:rdbms` — [Durable workflow engine R&D opportunities/ai_agents_wasm_edge.md:106-116](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/ai_agents_wasm_edge.md)
- Golem manual update uses user `save-snapshot` and `load-snapshot` — [correctness_dx_verification.md:34-44](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/correctness_dx_verification.md)
- Cloudflare: Agents are Durable Objects holding WebSocket connections and state; Workflows handle long tasks. No peer-reviewed Durable Objects paper was found — [ai_agents_wasm_edge.md:32](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/ai_agents_wasm_edge.md); [academic_research.md:64](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/academic_research.md)
- Academic lineage: Netherite (partitioning, recovery logs, asynchronous snapshots, speculative communication), Orleans actor-oriented transactions, Snapper — [academic_research.md:14, 37-39, 98-102](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/academic_research.md)
- Harvest positioning: "Postgres plus a hash-encoded shard id is a deliberate DBOS-like 'library plus Postgres' architecture. There is no log-structured or embedded-KV store like Restate's RocksDB, and no pluggable persistence trait." Missing primitives: virtual objects or keyed actors — [autumn_harvest_capability_map.md:32, 69, 284](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20R%26D%20opportunities/autumn_harvest_capability_map.md); "Virtual objects / durable entities: Absent" (written before #1975) — [harvest_programming_model.md:50, 279-287](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20feature%20gaps/harvest_programming_model.md)
- Agent convergence: "session = entity/actor/virtual object" — [market_trends_requirements.md:33](../Durable%20workflow%20engine%20feature%20gaps/market_trends_requirements.md)
- `docs/comparison.md` names Restate's single binary with embedded RocksDB storage and its Virtual Objects as reasons to choose Restate — [docs/comparison.md:72, 105, 403-406](../../docs/comparison.md)

### Inferences
- The proposal keeps Postgres as the log where Restate uses Bifrost. That keeps Harvest's transactional features (Q3) and changes only compute placement. It is the Temporal history-shard pattern on Postgres, with a Restate-style local materialization.
- The "programming model" note predates the keyed entity. Its "Absent" verdict for durable entities is now partially stale: sugar exists, a native primitive does not.

### Gaps
- No note in `research_notes/` gives Temporal shard-ownership fencing details (range IDs), Restate leader-election mechanics or Durable Objects' single-instance guarantee from primary sources. Another researcher on this topic may cover them.
